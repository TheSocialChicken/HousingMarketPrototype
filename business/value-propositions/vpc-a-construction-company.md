# Value Proposition Canvas — Segment A
## Construction Company (Procurement Team)
> AI-Powered Construction Procurement | Last updated: 2026-05-08

---

## Value Proposition Statement

> We help construction procurement teams stop wasting hours on manual supplier comparison and certificate chasing — by giving them a locally deployed AI, trained on their own data, that matches materials to spec, ranks suppliers, and produces approval-ready reports in seconds.

---

## Pitch Narrative (30 seconds)

*"Your procurement team spends hours every week digging through emails, Excel sheets, and supplier portals just to compare three quotes. When the best buyer leaves, that knowledge walks out the door. We deploy a local AI — trained on your own procurement history — that matches any specification to your suppliers, generates a comparison report in seconds, and explains every recommendation. Your data never leaves your building. The longer you use it, the smarter it gets."*

---

## Customer Profile

**Who:** Procurement managers, purchasing departments, project planners, and supplier relationship managers at mid-size construction companies (50–500 employees).

**Context:** Active project pipeline, multiple concurrent material procurement needs, working with a mix of preferred suppliers and spot purchases. Procurement is a core function but often under-resourced. Key knowledge often lives with one or two experienced buyers.

### Pains

| Pain | Severity | Detail |
|------|----------|--------|
| Supplier information is scattered — email, Excel, portals, phone | High | No single place to search; comparison requires manual aggregation |
| Comparing supplier offers is slow and error-prone | High | Manual work takes hours; errors cause wrong orders |
| Certificates expire and the gap is only discovered at the wrong moment | High | Certificate tracking is manual; chased per order |
| Procurement knowledge lives in one or two people's heads | High | Single point of failure; high risk when experienced buyer leaves |
| Hard to prove a procurement decision was right after the fact | Medium | Approval trail is informal; hard to reconstruct if challenged |
| ERP system doesn't help with supplier discovery or comparison | Medium | ERP tracks transactions, not intelligence or comparison logic |
| New buyers take months to reach full productivity | Medium | Onboarding relies on shadowing the expert, not a system |
| Preferred supplier lists become stale — better options go unnoticed | Medium | No mechanism to surface new qualified suppliers automatically |
| Concern about using cloud AI for sensitive pricing and supplier data | Medium | Procurement data is commercially sensitive |

## Qualification Criteria

A construction company is a good fit for a first pilot if they meet:
- [ ] Active project pipeline (not project-based / irregular)
- [ ] At least [TBD] months of procurement history in ERP or structured records
- [ ] 2+ buyers in the procurement team (makes knowledge-sharing gain tangible)
- [ ] Recurring material procurement (not one-off custom builds only)
- [ ] Decision-maker accessible (procurement director, CFO, or operations director)
