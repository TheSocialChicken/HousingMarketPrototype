## Slide 2 — The Problem (W setup)

**Screen headline:**
> "Construction procurement managers make million-euro decisions from Excel spreadsheets."

**Three points (short, no jargon):**
- Procurement teams spend 40% of their time on manual comparison work — emails, PDFs, supplier portals
- Every supplier choice is a documentation liability. Most decisions can't be reconstructed if challenged
- The AI tools that exist for this? All cloud-based. A Dutch municipality cannot legally use a single one of them

**Speaker notes:**

"I've sat with procurement managers at construction companies across the Netherlands. The story is always the same: three supplier emails, an Excel sheet they've been updating for six years, and a gut call that gets written up after the fact — if anyone ever asks.

For municipalities it's worse. They're legally accountable for every euro they spend. One auditor question they can't answer and there's a political problem. They know AI would help. They also know they can't touch it — because the moment data leaves their server, they're exposed."

## Slide 3 — What It Is (W)

**Screen headline:**
> "A fine-tuned AI procurement assistant. Runs on your server. Trained on your data."

**Three points:**
- Matches a procurement need to a ranked, reasoned list of qualified suppliers
- Runs locally — no data leaves the client's infrastructure, ever
- Learns from the client's own procurement history — gets more accurate over time, not less

**Speaker notes:**

"Think of it as a procurement expert who has read every contract your company has ever signed, knows every supplier you've ever worked with, remembers every certification and delivery failure — and writes the audit report automatically.

It doesn't live in our cloud. It lives in theirs. That's not a limitation — for our clients, that's the product."

## Slide 4 — How It Works (H)

**Screen headline:**
> "Three steps. One report. Full audit trail."

**Visual flow (simple, left to right):**

```

 Material, spec,         Ranked by price,          Reasoning, certifications,
 quantity, timeline      quality, lead time,        audit-ready — one click
```

**Speaker notes:**

"The procurement manager types in what they need — the material, specification, quantity, delivery window. The AI searches their supplier history, their current catalogue, current certifications. It returns a ranked list with reasoning attached to every result.

Then it generates a structured report — ready for manager approval, legal sign-off, or an auditor who shows up three years later.

The model improves with every decision. The more they use it, the better it knows their suppliers, their preferences, their standards. That's not a feature — that's why clients don't leave."

The question isn't whether this market exists. Parspec proved it. The question is who owns the EU version of it. We think that should be us — a team built from inside the Dutch construction and public sector ecosystem, with the university anchor and the municipality relationships already in place.

We're looking for a partner who sees that, not just a cheque."
