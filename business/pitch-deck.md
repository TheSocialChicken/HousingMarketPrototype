## Slide 2 - The Problem (W setup)

**Screen headline:**
> "Construction procurement managers make million-euro decisions from Excel spreadsheets."

**Three points (short, no jargon):**
- Procurement teams spend 40% of their time on manual comparison work - emails, PDFs, supplier portals
- Every supplier choice is a documentation liability. Most decisions can't be reconstructed if challenged
- The AI tools that exist for this? All cloud-based. A Dutch municipality cannot legally use a single one of them

**Speaker notes:**

"I've sat with procurement managers at construction companies across the Netherlands. The story is always the same: three supplier emails, an Excel sheet they've been updating for six years, and a gut call that gets written up after the fact - if anyone ever asks.

For municipalities it's worse. They're legally accountable for every euro they spend. One auditor question they can't answer and there's a political problem. They know AI would help. They also know they can't touch it - because the moment data leaves their server, they're exposed."

## Slide 3 - What It Is (W)

**Screen headline:**
> "A fine-tuned AI procurement assistant. Runs on your server. Trained on your data."

**Three points:**
- Structures procurement decisions - the AI ranks and documents, the procurement professional reviews and decides
- Runs locally and learns from the client's own data - gets more accurate with every procurement cycle, not less
- Not generative AI - a fine-tuned process tool trained on real procurement history; no hallucinated suppliers or certifications

**Speaker notes:**

"Think of it as a procurement expert who has read every contract your company has ever signed, knows every supplier you've ever worked with, remembers every certification and delivery failure - and structures the comparison for the manager to review.

The manager still decides. The AI does the retrieval, the ranking, the documentation - everything that currently takes three hours in a spreadsheet. It doesn't live in our cloud. It lives in theirs. That's not a limitation - for our clients, that's the product."

## Slide 4 - How It Works (H)

**Screen headline:**
> "Three steps. One report. Full audit trail."

**Visual flow (simple, left to right):**

```
[Describe the need]  →  [AI ranks and scores]  →  [Manager reviews]  →  [Decision documented]
 Material, spec,         Ranked by price,           Human checks,          Audit trail built
 quantity, timeline      quality, lead time,         approves or adjusts    at point of decision
                         certification, history
```

**Speaker notes:**

"The procurement manager types in what they need - the material, specification, quantity, delivery window. The AI searches their supplier history, their current catalogue, current certifications. It returns a ranked list with reasoning attached to every result.

Then it generates a structured report - ready for manager approval, legal sign-off, or an auditor who shows up three years later.

The model improves with every decision. The more they use it, the better it knows their suppliers, their preferences, their standards. That's not a feature - that's why clients don't leave."

## Slide 7 - Business Model (A)

**Screen headline:**
> "We deliver. They own. We're available when needed."

**Revenue per client:**

| | Construction co. | Municipality |
|---|---|---|
| Implementation (one-time) | €55,000 | €58,000 |
| Annual maintenance (optional) | €8,000 | €9,000 |
| Retraining engagement (as needed) | €12,000 | €12,500 |

No subscription. No platform dependency. No ongoing permission to use.

**Alternative:** Managed service tier for clients without IT infrastructure to self-host - higher cost, less control, client's choice.

**Key metrics:**
- LTV : CAC = **5.6×**
- Payback period: **< 6 months** (implementation fee alone is 2.3× CAC)
- Churn: **< 5%** (fine-tuned model = their institutional knowledge; leaving means losing it)

**Revenue path:** Workshop → Paid pilot → Implementation → Maintenance contract → Retraining on demand

**Speaker notes:**

"The standard model has no subscription. We build the system, we deploy it on their infrastructure, we hand it over. They own the fine-tuned model, the integration, everything. We get nothing for it to keep running.

What keeps us in the relationship is the maintenance contract - software updates, support SLA - and retraining engagements when they've accumulated enough new data to warrant a model refresh. Both are optional. Both are genuinely valuable. Neither is a condition for the system to function.

This is not SaaS. It's how this market already buys enterprise software - ERP implementations, IT deployments, consulting engagements. We fit into that procurement pattern naturally.

We also run procurement simulation workshops before the product is live. Revenue from day one. And those workshops are our best customer discovery channel."
