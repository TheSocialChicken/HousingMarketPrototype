## Shared Value Map Summary

Our offer has three layers that are relevant across all segments:

**1. Local AI with fine-tuned model**
Deployed on the client's own infrastructure. Trained on their procurement history. No data sent to external APIs. Gets more accurate over time.

**2. Specification-to-supplier matching + comparison report**
Takes a material specification and returns a ranked, multi-criteria comparison of matching suppliers - price, delivery, quality, certifications, reliability, sustainability. Output is audit-ready.

**3. Explainability**
Every comparison output includes a reasoning trail: which criteria drove the result, which data was used, where it came from. Designed for human review, management approval, and audit.

## Strategic Observations

**Segments A and B are the right dual focus at launch.**
Both have severe, quantified pain (€49,500/yr per PM in admin waste; McKinsey estimates 80% of large projects run over budget, with procurement failures a primary driver) and strong offer fit. The construction company is faster to close (1–3 months); the municipality opens a replicable market - 342 municipalities all face identical compliance obligations, so one reference unlocks many. Run them in parallel.

**CPR 2024 creates a new urgency hook for Segment B.**
The EU Construction Products Regulation 2024/3110 is operational from January 2026. Municipal procurement teams do not yet have tooling to handle CPR documentation at the supplier level. This is a specific, time-bound conversation that opens in Q2–Q3 2026 - before annual budget setting.

**Segment E (suppliers) is a data strategy, not a revenue strategy yet.**
Onboarding suppliers as data partners early - before a paid tier exists - builds the matching engine asset that makes segments A, B, and C more valuable. Start this now, unpaid, with suppliers relevant to first buyer clients.

**Segment C is the prize, but not the starting point.**
Sovereign IT fit is perfect. But the 12–24 month sales cycle and missing supply-chain-risk features make it a V2 target. Use a research partnership (Windesheim + infrastructure org) to build the reference and gather requirements.

**The AI adoption gap is an advantage, not a headwind.**
65% of construction decision-makers rate AI favorably but only 12% use it regularly - the gap is integration complexity and data privacy concerns, not skepticism. A locally deployed, pre-integrated product removes both barriers simultaneously. First mover in on-premise construction procurement AI in the Dutch market is a defensible position.

**The minimum training data threshold is a cross-segment qualification tool.**
Once established (a core research question for the KIEM grant), it tells you which prospects qualify for fine-tuning and which need a base model or lighter product tier. This threshold is different per segment - enterprise orgs have more data than project developers. 2 years of ERP history is the working assumption for Segment A qualification.
